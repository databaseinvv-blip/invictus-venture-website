How We Work

A four-stage engagement model.

We don't sell a service before we understand the problem. Every engagement begins with a conversation, not a quote. From there, we move from advice to proposal to execution to documentation — at the client's pace.

01
DISCOVERY

Conversation & Surface Review

We sit down with you to understand your current situation, your business condition, and what you're actually trying to solve. A surface-level due diligence — and honest advice on what you should do next. No fee. No pressure to engage.

02
PROPOSAL

Tailored Proposal & Refinement

Based on what you need, we draft a proposal scoped to the problem — not to a template. We meet, refine, adjust, and meet again until the scope, the approach, and the deliverables are right for your business.

03
EXECUTION

Execute & Negotiate Together

We execute the proposal alongside you — structuring the equity, modelling the dilution, negotiating the terms with co-founders, investors, family members, or key talent. You stay in control of every decision; we do the work to get you there.

04
DOCUMENTATION

Legal Black & White

Once the terms are agreed and the structure is settled, our corporate lawyer drafts and finalises the professional documentation — shareholder agreements, scheme rules, trust deeds, board resolutions. Signed, filed, defensible.

TYPE A · PILLAR 01

Co-Founder Equity Planning

Share. Rights. Gain. Engineered for the people who started the business.

Plan your cap table ›

We plan how co-founder equity should look across three dimensions — Share, Rights, and Gain. How shares are divided between co-founders. What rights each one holds. And how the gain — vesting, exit proceeds, control — tracks the effort each one actually puts in.

The goal is alignment. Each co-founder works in their strength area, their gain is calibrated to their contribution, and the whole team points at the same outcome: IPO or M&A, with the cap table intact when the deal arrives.

01
Share AllocationProportional to capital invested, effort committed, and future contribution — not split evenly by default.
02
Rights ArchitectureVoting, board seats, veto rights, information rights — defined per co-founder, written into the SHA.
03
Gain AlignmentVesting schedules, cliffs, milestone unlocks, exit waterfall — gain tracks effort, not signature date.
04
Strength-Area FocusFounder role definitions so each plays the position they win in, without ambiguity.
05
IPO / M&A ReadinessCap-table structure that survives a diligence room — drag-along, lock-up, deal mechanics.
06
Founder Buyback MechanicsDeparture provisions designed before they're needed — repurchase price, payment terms, IP clauses.
TYPE B · PILLAR 02

Investor Equity Planning

Bring capital in. Keep control. Compound the company, not the cap table.

Structure your raise ›

Banks lend you debt. Investors buy a piece of the company. The question isn't whether to take outside money — it's how to structure it so the capital accelerates growth without surrendering operations.

We design financing structures that answer four hard questions: how much equity to give, at what valuation, with what investor rights, and for what return. The end-state is a company that gets the fund it needs — and an investor whose influence stops at the boardroom door.

01
Equity Financing StructureCommon, preferred, convertible, SAFE — instrument matched to stage and investor type.
02
Pre-Money ValuationDefensible methodology, not a number plucked from the air. Backs up to diligence.
03
Dilution ModellingSeed → Series A → Series B scenarios with realistic terms — modelled before you sign.
04
Investor Rights CalibrationInformation rights, consent rights, anti-dilution, ROFR — sized to the cheque, not the ego.
05
ROI & Return ProfileReturns that compensate the risk taken — without strangling the company that has to deliver them.
06
Governance BoundaryWhere investor influence ends and operations begin. Written down, not assumed.
TYPE C · PILLAR 03

Talent Equity Planning

Equity for the red dots — the people who take revenue from 1mil to 10mil.

Identify your red dots ›

Some hires move the needle. Sales leaders. Business development heads. Founding GMs. The people fighting at the front of the market whose contribution to revenue is measurable to the ringgit — outlets opened, corporate accounts won, revenue lines built.

This pillar designs a structure that turns those people from commission earners into shareholders. They stop optimising for the next bonus cheque and start optimising for the overall company — through dividend, capital gain, and a real seat on the cap table.

Reserved for talent with direct, measurable revenue impact. Not all valuable employees fit here — that's what Pillar D is for.
01
Red-Dot IdentificationWho actually moves revenue. Specific, named, measurable — not symbolic recognition.
02
Direct-Impact Equity GrantsAllocation sized to revenue contribution potential, not tenure or title.
03
Commission-to-Equity TranslationConvert capped commission upside into uncapped equity upside in the company.
04
Performance VestingRevenue milestones, outlet counts, corporate accounts opened — vesting that tracks results.
05
Dividend + Capital Gain MechanicsIncome from operating performance. Upside on exit. Both, written in.
06
Goal RealignmentFrom quarterly commission bag to multi-year company value. Incentive structure rewires.
TYPE D · PILLAR 04

ESOS for SMEs

A share scheme designed for companies without a public market price.

Design your ESOS ›

Listed companies have a market that prices their shares every minute. SMEs don't — and that's why most off-the-shelf ESOS templates fail when SMEs try to use them. We design ESOS specifically for the non-listed SME context, with valuation methodology that holds up to scrutiny.

Where Pillar C rewards the red dots, this pillar covers everyone else who makes the company run — backend, operations, support, admin. Their contribution to revenue isn't directly measurable, but the sustainability of the business depends on them. Their equity should reflect that. Every employee subscribes; every employee's goal aligns with the company's.

01
Non-Listed ValuationDefensible share price for companies without a public market — our specialist methodology.
02
Whole-Company CoverageBackend, operations, support, admin — the unmeasurable work that keeps the company running.
03
Subscription MechanicsHow employees buy in. What they pay. When they pay. How it's administered.
04
Sustainability RewardEquity for the people whose contribution is to longevity, not to this quarter's number.
05
Vesting + Lock-UpStructures that retain without trapping. Departure mechanics designed in, not bolted on.
06
Exit Liquidity DesignSecondary windows, tender offers, redemption mechanics — so the scheme actually pays out.
TYPE E · PILLAR 05

Family Business Transition

Effort earns rights. Bloodline keeps dignity. The company keeps running.

Start a confidential review ›

Family business carries a kind of decision-making no other company has. Hierarchy by age sits next to hierarchy by contribution. Bloodline sits next to bargaining power. The uncle who built the brand sits across the table from the cousin who inherited shares but lives overseas. The son-in-law who has run operations for fifteen years has no equity. The patriarch is past retirement but still chairs every meeting.

Most family transitions fail not at the legal document — they fail because no one ever wrote down which rules of decision-making apply, and to whom. This pillar writes them down. Active contributors receive equity with operational rights — voting, board seats, day-to-day control. Non-active family members receive equity with economic rights — dividend, capital gain, beneficiary status — but not operational influence. Both sides keep their dignity.

Open only to family businesses — particularly those inheriting from an older generation or carrying multiple branches of family shareholders. Engagements are partner-led and confidential from receipt.
01
Family Constitution & CharterThe written rule-book. Who decides what. Voting thresholds. Dividend policy. Dispute mechanism. Ratified across generations.
02
Dual-Class Share StructureClass A (voting · operational · earned) vs Class B (non-voting · economic · inherited). The instrument that separates effort from inheritance.
03
Honorary & Emeritus RolesChairman Emeritus, Founder-in-Residence, Family Council Chair — positions that respect lifetime contribution without granting operational veto.
04
Generational Succession SequencingG1 → G2 → G3 paced over years. Phased transfer of board seats, signing authority, and operating responsibility.
05
In-Law & Operator RecognitionSons-in-law, daughters-in-law, and outside professionals who run operations get equity matched to their work.
06
Branch EqualisationWhen one family branch carries the operating load and others coast — mechanisms to keep both fair without breaking either.
07
Holding & Trust StructuresFamily Holdco, perpetual family trust, beneficial-ownership architecture. Cross-border designs for diaspora branches.
08
Exit & Buyout MechanicsFor family members who want out — independent valuation, payment terms, dignity intact.
09
Independent Mediation LayerDifficult conversations — partner-led, confidential, neutral. The discussion the family has been postponing.
10
Family Council & AssemblyTwo-tier governance: Council for operating-business decisions; Assembly for wealth, legacy, and next-generation matters.
